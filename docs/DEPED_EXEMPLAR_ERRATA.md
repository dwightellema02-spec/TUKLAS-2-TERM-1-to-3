# Errors found in the DepEd Grade 7 Lesson Exemplars (Quarter 1, Lessons 1 to 6)

While writing the Week 1 to Week 6 lessons for Tuklas, each worked example and answer key in the six DepEd Lesson Exemplars
(Mathematics 7, Quarter 1, Lessons 1 to 6, SY 2024-2025) was re-checked by calculation. The items below did not check out.
**This is a list for a teacher to confirm, not a ruling.** Each item names the lesson and the example so it can be found.
The Tuklas lessons do not copy these passages and teach the corrected version.

| # | Lesson | Where | What the exemplar says | What the calculation gives |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Lesson 1 (Week 1) | Activity 3, Drawing 1, 3 and 4 (pentagon, octagon, decagon) | A circle of radius 2 cm (pentagon) or 3 cm (octagon, decagon) is divided into equal parts and the points joined, and the answer says "side length is 2 cm / 3 cm each". | Joining points on a circle of radius R gives a side of 2R·sin(180° ÷ n). Pentagon, R = 2 cm: about 2.35 cm. Octagon, R = 3 cm: about 2.30 cm. Decagon, R = 3 cm: about 1.85 cm. The side equals the radius only for the regular hexagon (Drawing 2 is right). |
| 2 | Lesson 1 (Week 1) | Activity 3, Drawing 4 (decagon), Step 2 | "Divide the circle into eight equal parts … every 36 degrees". | 360° ÷ 36° = 10 equal parts, not eight. |
| 3 | Lesson 2 (Week 2) | Formative assessment, part II, figures 3 and 5 | The bow-tie shape and the five-pointed star are classified "Non Convex" polygons. | Their sides cross each other, so by the exemplar's own definition in Lesson 1 (sides meet only at their endpoints) they are not polygons. |
| 4 | Lesson 3 (Week 3) | "You Do" question 1 | If m∠6 = 107°, the answer key gives the adjacent angle as 83°. | An exterior angle and its adjacent interior angle add up to 180°, so 180° − 107° = 73°. |
| 5 | Lesson 3 (Week 3) | Example 5(a), guided practice | Five angles of a hexagon are 100°, 110°, 120°, 130° and 140°; the answer is 240°. | A hexagon's angles add up to 720°. The five angles add up to 600°, so the sixth is 720° − 600° = 120°. |
| 6 | Lesson 3 (Week 3) | Example 2, step 3 | Asks for "the sum of the interior angles of a nonagon" for a five-sided figure. | The figure is a pentagon (the working correctly uses n = 5, 540°). |
| 7 | Lesson 3 (Week 3) | Example 6 (We Do) | The working repeats "38° + 60° + m∠4 = 180°" from Example 3 and ends with m∠4 = 82°, but the question asks for m∠7. | The working does not match the question. |
| 8 | Lesson 5 (Week 5) | Simple interest, Example 3 | P = 5000 ÷ (0.05)(6) is written as 5000 ÷ 0.03. | (0.05)(6) = 0.3, so P = 5000 ÷ 0.3 = ₱16,666.67. The final answer is right; the middle line has a slip. |
| 9 | Lesson 6 (Week 6) | Solving problems involving rates, Example 3 | The conclusion reads "Bill can hit golf balls in 23 hours", with the number left out. | The working gives 437 golf balls. |
| 10 | Lesson 2 (Week 2) | Formative assessment, part III, item 4 | The options and the answer key print "180∘180∘". | A repeated symbol (typing slip). Meant: 180°. |

Items 1 to 5 can mislead a student, so they matter most. Items 6 to 10 are slips in the working or in the wording.

Everything else that was re-computed (the interior angle sums, the percentage increase and decrease tables, the discount, commission, sales tax
and simple interest examples, and the rate and speed answer keys) agreed with the exemplars.

How this was checked: each number was recomputed by hand and by program from the figures printed in the exemplar. The pictures were not
measured, so an error that appears only in a drawing would not have been found. A teacher should still read the originals.
